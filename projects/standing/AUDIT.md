# Project 22 — T3 pragmatics audit

*KsK T3: the regime of a core proposition, the translation test, new statements, the order-words of the field and the pass-words beneath
them (ATP 76-82, 110, 145-148, via KsK §4.2 and ch. 6). Failure: the translation test changes only the wording, not the project's design.
Each entry dated; earlier entries stand.*

## Entry 1 — 2026-11-04, session 1, written before the count

**The proposition.** The checklist's: "*X* is a synonym of *Y*." Its speaker is an institution speaking for a consensus ("a global
consensus view", Govaerts et al. 2021); its grammar is the code of nomenclature (priority from 1753, types, legitimacy), which this
project does not quote and will not paraphrase beyond what the columns show.

**Regime.** Signifying with a postsignifying edge (ATP 147, via KsK T3): every name refers to a centre (the code, the type specimen),
and a judgment of synonymy is a verdict — the judge's sentence that turns the accused into a convict (ATP 80-81) — dated, instantaneous,
and touching no body: the plant stays as it was, a name stops being its name. The first publication was already such a transformation:
a specimen became a species on a page.

**Translation test, planned.** The count translates the verdicts out of their regime — from a ruling per name into a curve per year:
what share of each year's acts of naming still stand. What passes (ATP 147): whether a year's naming survives as a body, independent of
any one name. What will not pass, stated now: the reason a name fell (the argument in a monograph), which no column holds.

**Order-words of the field, named now.** "Accepted"; "Synonym"; "Illegitimate"; "Unplaced" (a name the consensus has not ruled on — a
verdict withheld). **Pass-word to look for** (ATP 110): the basionym, the epithet that passes from one name into the next and survives the
verdict on its first name — the component of passage through which a sunk name keeps standing in another.

**Failure watch.** If the curve only redescribes the verdicts and changes nothing in session 2's design, T3 has triggered.

## Entry 1, continued — after the count (02:09 UTC)

**What passed.** The translation test held one thing and broke another. It held the curve: each year's naming does survive as a body,
and the bodies differ by an order of magnitude — 52 % of 1753's species names stand, 2 % of 1796's, 6 % of 1891's, 76 % of the 2020s'.
What it broke is the registered grammar: the verdict "moved" (homotypic) was coded `T`, not `TRUE`, so the registered count could not
tell moving from merging (P6 and P7 not testable). The order-word was there; I had misheard its spelling.

**The pass-word, found.** The basionym does what entry 1 guessed: 28 % of standing species names stand on an epithet first published under
another name, a median 56 years earlier. And read exploratorily with the flag as `T`, the undone names split by age: of 1753's undone
names three in four were *moved* (the epithet passed on into another genus), of the nineteenth century's about one in three, of the
2020s' more than four in five. Nineteenth-century names fell by being merged; 1753's and today's by being moved. A sunk name that was moved still
stands, as an epithet, inside another name; a merged name is gone.

**Consequence for session 2's design (the failure watch).** The registered question asked "how much stands"; the count says the better
question is "in what form does it stand" — whole, as an epithet passed on, or not at all. Session 2 is registered on that three-way
split, and on the archived releases (v10 to now), where each verdict is datable to a release. That is a change of design, not of
wording: T3 has not triggered.

## Entry 2 — 2026-11-05, session 2, after the count

**The order-word, dated.** Entry 1 read "*X* is a synonym of *Y*" as a judge's sentence. Seven releases let the sentence be caught between
two statements that bracket it: 39,436 species names (3.9 % of those present throughout) carry a different verdict in June 2026 than in
December 2022. 9,789 were condemned (stood, then not), 4,418 acquitted (undone, then stood). The incorporeal transformation (ATP 80-81,
via KsK §4.2) is visible here as what it is: nothing in any plant moved.

**Translation test.** The verdict per name became a flow between forms per release. What passed: the direction of the field's attention —
by decade of publication, the share of 2022's standing names undone by 2026 climbs from about 1.5 % for the eighteenth century to 3.9 %
for the 2020s, and the share of undone names restored climbs faster (5.9 % for the 2020s, from 829 names). What did not pass, and what
the test found instead: **a third of the changes are not verdicts on plants at all.** At least 13,555 changes keep the same accepted name;
9,739 are merged → moved, the synonym re-described as sharing its type. The record re-spelled its own order-words. Entry 1 said I had
misheard the flag's spelling; tonight the record shows it has been re-hearing its own.

**Consequence for session 3 (the failure watch).** Session 1's "moved or merged" split by decade (section 4 of the page) rests on a flag
the record is still filling in; it cannot be read as the field's judgment of each period without that caveat, now on the page. For session 3
the design changes: a change of verdict is two kinds of act — **a re-placement** (the name points elsewhere: an act on the plant's
standing) and **a re-description** (same accepted name: an act on the record). Any work counts the first as the verdict and shows the second
as the record's own grammar, not mixed. That is a change of design, not of wording: T3 has not triggered.

**Pass-word, again.** The basionym was entry 1's component of passage. Tonight's is the release: the same name passing through seven
statements, each a little different. A name is not standing; it is standing *as of* a release.

## Entry 3 — 2026-11-06, session 3, the work

**The translation, tested on the picture.** Entry 2 split a change of verdict into two acts — a re-placement (on a plant's standing) and a
re-description (on the record) — and asked any work to keep them apart. The work is that translation carried into a form: a dot per change,
at the year of the name. The danger the registration named (`SESSION3.md`, instruments): that a dot flattens both back into one "change".
The design answers it in three places, each a decision, not a wording: re-descriptions are not drawn until asked for; when asked, they go
below the line, in grey, never stacked with the verdicts; and the counts that make the page's opening claim ("27,429 verdicts") exclude
them. The count shows the split was worth making: re-descriptions are 34 % of all changes and 75 % of them fall in the first two intervals,
so a picture that mixed them would have shown the court busiest at the start, when it was mostly the record re-spelling itself.

**What did not pass.** The class "sent" is itself mixed (adversarial read (a)): a synonym whose accepted name was sunk is "sent" though no
one ruled on it, and a synonym re-pointed without a change of kind is not counted at all. The order-word "Synonym" hides two speakers —
an editor ruling on this name, and a cascade from a ruling on another. The work says so in words; it cannot draw it.

**The order-word, last.** Entry 1 read the verdict as a judge's sentence (ATP 80-81, via KsK §4.2). The title keeps the figure and the
page undoes it in its last lines (a database, kept by editors, stating a consensus view). That is the minor usage tried once: the court's
vocabulary put in variation against a record that is not a court.

**Failure watch.** T3 fails if the translation changes only the wording. It changed the work's design (what is drawn by default, where the
re-descriptions go, what the headline counts). **Not triggered.**
