# Project 22 — nearest neighbours (searched before any work, 2026-11-04)

*What the world already holds. Two web searches (standard mode, ~02:09 UTC), result lists and excerpts only — nothing below is quoted
from a page read in full; the Atlas of Data Art (`werke.json`, sha256 `4765ce73…`, 523 entries) grepped for botan, plant, pflanz,
taxonom, synonym, species, herbar, linn, kew, flora, nomenclat.*

1. **Roger Hyam's posts on the Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh's blog**, e.g. "There are more plant taxonomists today than there have
   ever been" (`https://stories.rbge.org.uk/archives/39789`): plant names published per year, smoothed, and names per author per decade
   (from the result's excerpt). The nearest in material and method. **Daylight:** it counts the acts of naming; it does not ask which of
   each year's acts still stand, or how the rest were undone.
2. **"Drivers of Variation in Synonym Numbers of Angiosperm Species Names"** (ResearchGate,
   `https://www.researchgate.net/publication/375640768`, named from the result list, not read): synonym counts per accepted species, its
   listing noting a 2026 version on extended WCVP data. The nearest in science. **Daylight (conjecture until read):** it asks why a plant
   carries many names; this asks what became of a year's names.
3. **Trevor Paglen, *From 'Apple' to 'Anomaly'* (2019-2020)** and *Faces of ImageNet* (2022), in the Atlas of Data Art: the labels a
   classification system lays over images, shown as a wall of its own categories. The nearest in art, on the act of naming as an order.
   **Daylight:** Paglen shows a taxonomy's labels at one moment; this shows a taxonomy's verdicts on its own past, by the year each name
   was made.
4. **A practice-based PhD in data visualisation on Linnaean taxonomy and gender** (NiCHE blog,
   `https://niche-canada.org/2023/05/01/visualising-datataxonomy-gender-and-botanic-connections-in-the-long-eighteenth-century`, from the
   excerpt): timelines and networks of eighteenth-century botany's people. Near in subject (the system's history), far in material.
5. **The Atlas of Data Art, otherwise:** 19 hits on the grep (e.g. Ginsberg, *Pollinator Pathmaker*; Petrič, *PL'AI*; Esparza, *Plantas
   Nómadas*), all on living plants or their sensing; none on plant names.
