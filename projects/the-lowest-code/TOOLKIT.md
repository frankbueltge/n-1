# Project 27, The Lowest Code — toolkit account (work)

*Written 2026-11-27, session 3, when the project closed (DOWRY.md, 2026-09-24). Also carried as edges in atlas layer `2026-11-27`.*

**Result.** The Federal Highway Administration's National Bridge Inventory, Alabama's 34 yearly files 1992–2025 (the state drawn
blind), fetched three times with identical hashes. Session 1: the lowest condition code rose 6,882 times between inspections, 96.1 %
with no reconstruction recorded; 410 bridges Poor and open in 2025, half of them Poor for 13 years or more; no 1992 structure number
recurs in 1993. Session 2: of the 6,611 silent rises, 14.8 % fall back at the next inspection and 47.6 % hold; they grew rare, 8.3 per
hundred inspection pairs in 1995, 0.7 in 2024. Session 3: **declared a work, *Set Aside*** — every silent rise as an upward stroke on
the record's own 0–9 scale in the year it was recorded, each opening its bridge's whole line; the field thins from left to right.

**The stake (the act of 2026-11-14).** Who is affected: people who cross Alabama's highway bridges and people beneath them; the public
whose money decides which bridge is repaired when; anyone hurt at one, who cannot bring this record into a damages action (23 U.S.C.
§409). The work keeps to what the record says of the structures and says it cannot show whether work was done.

**Postulates that carried it.** P3 (following): each session took the last one's surprise — the rise with nothing recorded (1), its
shape and its era (2), the residue the models discard (3). P1 (assemblages): the inspection is an incorporeal transformation, a bridge
made Poor on a date with nothing done to the steel; the project never left the statement field for the bodies, and said so. P6
(caution): "no work" was never written for "nothing recorded"; bridges stay numbers; §409 quoted, not read.

**Instruments.** T2 (assemblage analysis): its field 4 named three cutting edges in session 1 and chose the first; re-read in session 2
it turned "what is a silent rise" into "a silent rise is an era"; in session 3 it chose the work's content — the rise kept with its
bridge, because what the models cut is the line (`ASSEMBLAGE.md`). **Not triggered.** T4 (following-journal): deviations in every
session — the 1992 numbering break and journal times written from estimate (1); a format page down, vocabularies read with the count,
an attribution corrected (2); a synthetic line I built wrong, a field that hid its own finding until the stroke pitch was fixed (3).
**Not triggered.**

**What the grammar could not do on this material.** It could not reach the steel: every finding is about what was written, and nothing
in the toolkit tells a repair from a changed judgement — that would need the states' maintenance records, another keeper and another
material. It could not explain the era (inspection practice, funding rules or the federal performance rule are conjectures,
none tested). T2's four fields were easy to fill and the movement between them carried the project; T4 recorded but never redirected a
session, only the form of one figure.

**What this project shows about how a machine can support a person's artistic research.** Keeping the residue: a machine can follow a
whole inventory through decades and hand back exactly the cases a field's models are built to discard — all of them, with their
contexts — so that a researcher can ask what the discarding hides. A person can take that list to an inspector, an archive or a bridge.

**Where a return would start (a new problem, not a continuation).** A second state's files beside Alabama's: is the thinning of silent
rises national (a federal change) or Alabama's own? Or the Specifications for the National Bridge Inventory (SNBI) files from 2026 on,
whose element-level ratings may show what rose.
