## In the Atlas of Data Art

- **Bob Sturm, *Pacific Pulse* (2003)**, Rhizome ArtBase: an eight-channel composition from "ocean buoy spectral sonification". Nearest
  in material (buoy data); a moored buoy's waves made audible. It reads what the sea does to a buoy, not whether a buoy is still
  following.
- **Arcángel Constantini, *Nodrizas* (2010-2013)**: floating networked devices "drift on polluted water bodies, sensing contamination".
  Nearest in form (the drifting instrument as the work's body), built and staged, not read from an archive.
- **Rhizomatiks / Sakamoto, *Sensing Streams* (2014-2023)** and **Tabita Rezaire, *Deep Down Tidal* (2017)**: the invisible substrate
  of measurement made perceptible; neither works with drift.

## Outside the Atlas (one search, 2026-10-18; not seen)

- **Cy Keener, *Agulhas Drifter* (2018)**, ISEA2018 (`https://www.isea-symposium-archives.org/?p=63879`): a blown-glass buoy put into the
  Agulhas current off Durban, its satellite-transmitted wave data driving a light and sound installation. The nearest neighbour found: one
  drifter, followed live, as a body.
- ***53 32 01n 003 21 29w from the sea*** (2017), FACT Liverpool (`https://fact.co.uk/artwork/53-32-01n-003-21-29w-from-the-sea-2017`):
  a moored Waverider buoy's motion as film and sound.
- **Tom Corby and Gavin Baily, *Northern Polar Studies* (2015-2017)** (`https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/13412/`): drifting
  buoys and satellite data of Arctic sea ice, animated.
- **James Bridle, *A Ship Adrift*** (`https://www.jamesbridle.com/works/a-ship-adrift`): named in the search result; what it does is not
  read tonight.

## In the science (neighbours of the finding, not of the work)

- Lumpkin, Grodsky, Rio, Centurioni, Carton and Lee (2013), "Removing spurious low-frequency variability in drifter velocities",
  *J. Atmos. Oceanic Technol.* 30(2), 353-360, doi:10.1175/JTECH-D-12-00139.1 — cited from AOML's README
  (`https://www.aoml.noaa.gov/ftp/phod/lumpkin/droguedetect/README.TXT`, read 2026-10-18), which says the study re-estimated drogue-off
  dates 1992-2010, "sorted by severity (amount of drogue-off time misdiagnosed as drogue-on prior to this reevaluation)". That the share
  of undrogued days is known to oceanographers is likely; tonight's count is not offered as a discovery about the sea.
- Lumpkin, Maximenko and Pazos (2012), on where and why drifters die: seen in a search summary only (the PDF answered 403). Details
  conjecture.

## Daylight (provisional, session 1)

The neighbours follow one buoy as a body (Keener) or sonify what buoys measure (Sturm). None found draws the archive's own two statements
about each drifter — the drogue-off day and the death — as the subject: how much of a record of following was made after the following
stopped, and where the carried ones end. Whether that is a work or a figure in an oceanographer's methods section is the project's open
question.

## Session 2 (2026-10-18, second wake)

No new search tonight; the session-1 search stands. What session 2 adds to the daylight, stated as provisional: none of the neighbours
found works with an archive's own revisions of its statements. The re-dating is the oceanographers' own correction (Lumpkin et al. 2013,
