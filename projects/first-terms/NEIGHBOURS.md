# Project 24 — nearest neighbours

*Searched before any work (the works condition): the Atlas of Data Art (`https://frankbueltge.de/atlas/werke.json`, 688 entries, sha256
`b9d26292…`, fetched 2026-11-11) searched for OEIS, integer, Sloane, Folge, prime, Primzahl, mathemat, number theory — three hits, none on
integer sequences; two web searches (standard mode, 2026-11-11). Items below were seen as search results or through a fetch tool's
summary, as marked; none was read in full. To be searched again before any work is declared.*

1. **"Minimum number of terms to uniquely identify a sequence"**, OEIS wiki
   (`https://oeis.org/wiki/Minimum_number_of_terms_to_uniquely_identify_a_sequence`; read through a fetch summary). **Nearest in
   question.** By the summary: a rule of thumb ("you should enter six terms in the search box in order to obtain a manageably small
   plurality of results"), hand-gathered examples "as of 2020", most sections empty, the matching rule unstated (the search box matches a
   run anywhere in a sequence, not only its beginning). Daylight, as far as read: this study counts every sequence against every other,
   by its beginning, on the file of 2026-11-10, and counts the sequences the shown terms cannot tell apart at all.
2. **The strong law of small numbers** (R. K. Guy, 1988; OEIS wiki page `https://oeis.org/wiki/Strong_law_of_small_numbers`, seen as a
   search result, not read): "a warning against drawing conclusions based on the observation of a few small numbers" (search summary's
   wording). The idea this project measures; not a measurement of the encyclopedia.
3. **Gauvrit, Delahaye, Zenil, "Sloane's Gap"** (arXiv:1101.4470, seen as a search result): the distribution of how often each integer
   occurs in the OEIS. A study of the numbers, not of the sequences' beginnings.
4. **Durkin and Xie, a graph of the OEIS's cross-references** (RPI course project, `https://www.cs.rpi.edu/~cutler/classes/visualization/S20/final_projects/durkin_xie.pdf`,
   seen as a search result): sequences as nodes, references as edges. Whole-database, but the edges are the editors' references, not
   shared beginnings.
5. **OEIS pictures page** (`https://oeis.org/OEIS_pics.html`, seen as a search result) and visualisers of single sequences (Numberscope,
   ICERM; Bridges papers): pictures of sequences one at a time.

No data artwork found that draws the encyclopedia by where its sequences part.
