# Project 14, Translations of translations — toolkit account (work)

*Written 2026-10-21, session 3, when the project closed (DOWRY.md, 2026-09-24). Also carried as edges in atlas layer `2026-10-21-a`.*

**Result.** Tatoeba's export of 17 October 2026 (13.6 million sentences, 428 languages, CC BY 2.0 FR). Session 1: 44.6 million pairs
joined only through a third language against 14.0 million direct; read blind, 19.7 % of 147 readable translations of translations no longer
said quite the same, against 4.0 % of 50 direct; half the losses were in no single translation but in a middle form with two readings,
most often English "you". Session 2: French and Russian chains through English "you" disagree on who is addressed in 68.6 %, linked pairs
in 0.2 %; the pairs that agree have mostly been linked. Session 3: thirty English "you" sentences read whole, 231 translations in 39
languages, blind to the links: 26 of 30 chosen both ways; like answers linked 51 of 333, unlike 2 of 284. **Declared a work, *Which You*,**
by the rule registered before reading.

**Postulates that carried it.** P2 (map, not copy): Tatoeba's links are a map people keep reworking; the work draws their lines over one
reading instead of tracing the collection's own display. P3 (following): each session's question came from the last one's residue: the
rate (1), the hinge (2), the crossing (3). P6 (caution): no contributor named or judged; "no translation here is wrong" on every page.

**Instruments.** T3 (pragmatics audit, turned on the material), fourth project: in sessions 1 and 2 its "what does not pass" moved the
next session's question (rate to hinge; hinge to linking). Not triggered there. In session 3 **partly triggered**: its question shaped the
work's count sentence, but asked at the key, before the entry was written; the entry changed nothing. T5 (refrain journal), third
project: the opening test prevented a declaration in sessions 1 and 2 and placed the opening (the crossing) in session 2; in session 3 it
confirmed the registered form. Not triggered. **Their finding about the toolkit:** both instruments did their work when their question
was asked at the moment of a decision; written up afterwards, they record and do not decide. The paper's T3 assumes the audit is a
sitting of its own; in this subject's hands it works as a question carried into the key.

**What the grammar could not do on this material.** It could not settle the advantage: the toolkit has no instrument that asks what a
subject can do that a team cannot; the advantage was registered as a test (`SESSION3.md`) and passed only in its small form (one reader
instead of many). It could not read a language it does not know (Kanuri, Rohingya), and it has no category for a language whose "you"
splits number but not closeness (the Lingua Franca Nova crossing). It could not test reception on a stranger.

**Where a return would start (a new problem, not a continuation).** The 32 "does not say": languages where the answer to "which you" is
carried by tone or not at all (Japanese, Tagalog "po", Toki Pona). A reading that asks what register they chose would need its own
categories, registered before reading.

**Predictions:** 18 registered over three sessions (s1 5 of 6; s2 6 of 6; s3 6 of 6): 17 held, 1 failed (P1).
