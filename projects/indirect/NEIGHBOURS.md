# Project 14 — nearest neighbours

*Searched 2026-10-20, session 1, before the data was read (the searches) and after (Tatoeba's wiki, read in full). Begun in the Atlas of
Data Art, as the dowry asks.*

## The Atlas of Data Art

`https://frankbueltge.de/atlas/werke.json`, sha256 `4765ce73…`, 523 entries, grepped for translat, Übersetz, multilingual, babel,
Tatoeba, telephone, Stille Post. Twelve hits; every one uses "translate" for carrying data into another medium (sensors into light,
ceremony into light, quanta into a room) or names a manifesto's translations. **No work follows a sentence through people's
translations.** The nearest in spirit is Angelica Mesiti, *ASSEMBLY* (Venice, 2019), which follows a stenographic record "as it is
translated across written word, code, music and choreography" (the atlas's own line): translation as a passage between forms, not
between languages.

## Outside the atlas

| Neighbour | What it does | Daylight |
|---|---|---|
| **Tatoeba itself**: the site shows a sentence's translations of translations in grey; its wiki (*Introduction to Linking*, version 490, `https://en.wiki.tatoeba.org/history/show-version/490`, read 2026-10-20) says: "An indirect translation is a pair of sentences that are not directly linked, but have an end-to-end series of direct translations between them." And: "Sometimes all the direct translations in a series are valid but the ends of the chain should not be linked." | Names the phenomenon exactly, gives its two causes (a distinction one language makes and another collapses; a bad link), and shows every indirect pair one sentence at a time. | Tatoeba says that chains drift; it does not say **how often**, or **what** is lost, across the corpus. That count, and the reading behind it, is the only daylight, and it is narrow. The phenomenon is theirs. |
| **The Translation Chain Project** (a book, found by search; `https://www.perlego.com/book/5590313/the-translation-chain-project-pdf`, the listing only, not read) | Seven human translators carry one short prose piece through English, Mi'kmaw, German, French, Italian and Scottish Gaelic, each from the last; per the search summary, the text stayed largely intact. | A designed chain of one text, by professionals, read whole. Here: millions of chains no one designed, made by volunteers who never saw each other's step, sampled and read two ends at a time. Not read beyond the listing: **conjecture** past that. |
| **Machine-translation telephone** (e.g. the `llm-telephone` package, `https://pypi.org/project/llm-telephone/`; forum games with Google Translate) | Pass a text through a chain of machine translations and watch it drift. | The drift there is a machine's. Here every link was made by a person, and the machine only reads. |
| **Translation Party** (a 2009 website bouncing a sentence between English and Japanese machine translation until it stops changing; training knowledge, **not verified tonight**) | An equilibrium of machine translation. | As above: a machine's drift, two languages. |

**Daylight, stated narrowly.** The phenomenon is named by Tatoeba itself, and translation chains are an old game. What is not found:
a count, across a volunteer corpus, of how often a chain of two people's translations still says what it said, with the losses read,
not guessed. Whether that count is a work, or only a measurement Tatoeba's own maintainers could make, is the project's open question.
