## Clauses, fixed before reading

The five dispute-settlement clauses that the treaties themselves allow a party to refuse — **CERD 22, CEDAW 29, CAT 30, CRMW 92,
CED 42** — and one committee clause a party may refuse by declaration, **CAT 20** (refused under CAT 28, so a reference to article 28
counts as one to article 20). The other treaties (ICCPR, ICESCR, CRC, CRPD) have no such clause and are not read. Opt-in clauses (a state
accepting a committee, e.g. CERD 14, CAT 21 and 22, CED 31 and 32) hold nothing back and are not counted. **Comparator:** every other
article of the same five treaties, counted by the same rule, so that the comparison is page against page, today against today.

## Definitions

- **Section:** a state's own reservations-and-declarations entry on the treaty's page (`treaties.un.org`, MTDSG IV-2, 8, 9, 13, 16) —
  not the objections section, and not a section of declarations accepting a committee's competence. How the page separates them is
  read when the page is fetched; the separation rule is committed with the count, and any section that cannot be assigned is counted.
- **Names article N:** the section text contains "article N", or "paragraph … of article N", or "articles" followed, within the same
  sentence and 80 characters, by N as a whole number in a list. Applied identically to every article number.
- **Withdrawn (machine):** a footnote referenced from the state's name or section that contains a form of *withdraw*, does not contain
  *objection*, and names article N by the same rule.
- **Held back now (machine):** the section names N and no withdrawal footnote names N. **Withdrawn:** a withdrawal footnote names N.
  **Not:** neither. Counted per party; a party is counted once per article.
- Parties: the participants table's rows with a date of ratification, accession or succession. Pages are fetched to scratch for personal
  use and never committed (UN terms of use, `SELECTION.md` candidate B). Committed: counts, state and treaty names, article numbers and my
  readings — no wording.
