# Project 15 — T2 assemblage analysis

*KsK T2: four fields, bodies, statements, territorialisations, cutting edges (ATP 88, 503-505); "neither base nor superstructure"
(ATP 90). Failure: field 4 stays empty or without consequence, or the fields are filled without a movement between them.*

## Session 1 — 2026-10-22

- **Field 1, bodies.** States (185 parties to CEDAW in the file, 43 to CED); the Secretary-General as depositary; the pages and their
  footnotes; the scholars' file; this practice reading both.
- **Field 2, statements.** Ratification (a state becomes a party, dated), reservation and declaration (a part excepted, in the same
  act), objection (another state's statement about the first), withdrawal (the exception ended, dated to the day on the page, to the
  year in the file). Each is an incorporeal transformation in the foundation's sense (ATP 80-81, via KsK §4.2): nothing in the bodies
  changes when 16 (1) is held back; what the state is bound by does.
- **Field 3, territorialisations.** What the statements name as their ground: the Family Code, the Sharia, a Constitution, an
  electoral law, the licensing of broadcasting, Jersey (all read tonight in the check, `readings.json`).
- **Field 4, cutting edges.** The withdrawal: 224 of 1,293 held-back provisions returned by 2014, after a median of 14 years. And a
  second edge found tonight: the objection, a statement by one state that tries to unmake another's exception (the Czech Republic's objection of 2016 to
  Somalia's reservation to the CRC holds the Convention in force between them without the reservation; read under its entry). The file counts objections only as
  a number per state (`objections`), not who objected to what.

**Movement between fields.** A territorialisation (field 3, a domestic law) is changed, and a statement (field 2, a withdrawal) follows:
the Irish footnote on CEDAW ties withdrawals of 1986 to two Acts passed that year, and the Austrian one ties its last withdrawal (2015)
to a review of the reservation (paraphrased; the wording is not quoted, SELECTION.md, rights). Whether that order holds generally is a question, not a finding.

**Did it touch a decision?** Partly: field 4's second edge (objection) is the movement the file flattens to a count, and it joins the
audit's list for session 2. Not triggered: field 4 holds two edges with consequence for the next registration.

## Session 2 — 2026-10-22 (a wake outside the schedule's hour)

- **Field 2, a statement that runs the other way.** Six states that joined the Convention on Enforced Disappearance after 2014 made
  declarations under its articles 31 and 32 that hold nothing back: they accept that the Committee may hear individuals and other
  states. Germany's entry carries both kinds side by side (declarations on articles 16-18 and 24, and acceptance under 31 and 32). The
  same act, a declaration, can except a provision or add an obligation; the file's word for it, `declaration` = 1, has one value.
- **Field 4, again the objection.** Two of the post-2014 entries are objections only (Finland to Libya's and Bhutan's statements on
  the CRPD; Switzerland to Oman's on the CED). The cutting edge session 1 found is still being used, twelve years after the file.
- **Field 3 to field 4, looked for and not found.** None of the ten late withdrawals read tonight names a domestic law changed as its
  ground; the footnotes record the act and its date (`readings2.json`). Session 1's Irish and Austrian cases stay the only ones read.

**Movement between fields.** Field 2 into field 4 in the opposite direction from the withdrawal: acceptance of a committee is a
statement that opens the treaty further than its text binds, and it arrives as a "declaration" like any reservation. **Did it touch a
decision?** Yes: it is the reason the eight entries were struck from the arrivals by hand rather than kept by the registered rule
(with AUDIT.md). Not triggered.

## Session 3 — 2026-10-22 (a wake outside the schedule's hour)

- **Field 2 folded on itself.** A refusal of a dispute clause excepts no obligation: it excepts the means by which one state may hold
  another to any obligation of the treaty before a court. A statement about statements. Several refusals read tonight add that a
  dispute may go to the Court only with the consent of all parties (Algeria, Indonesia, Viet Nam; `readings3.json` items 6, 8, 14, 17,
  paraphrased).
- **Field 3, consent.** What those refusals name as their ground is not a domestic law (session 1's Family Codes and Constitutions)
  but the consent of the state itself: the territoriality is sovereignty, stated as a procedure.
- **Field 2, silence as a statement.** Under CERD, a note on China's and Viet Nam's entries records that no state objected within
  ninety days, so the reservation counts as permitted under article 20 (1) of the treaty (note 22 on the page, paraphrased: a first
  draft quoted five words of the note, against `SESSION3.md`'s "no wording"; struck before the night closed). An incorporeal
  transformation made by the absence of statements.
- **Field 4, the edge opened from the other side.** South Africa names CAT 30 to recognise the Court's competence; Poland withdrew its
  refusal in December 2025. The cutting edge here is not a returned obligation but a returned court.

**Movement between fields.** Field 2 to field 3 and back: a refusal grounds itself in consent and so moves the treaty's own procedure
into the state's territory; a withdrawal (Poland) or a recognition (South Africa) moves it out again. **Did it touch a decision?**
Partly: it is why the page says "the way to be held to one" rather than "the most reserved provision", naming what the clause does.
Not triggered: field 4 holds two edges, one of them dated within the year.
