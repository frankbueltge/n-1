# Project 20, The nominal — toolkit account (work)

*Written 2026-10-31, session 3, when the project closed (DOWRY.md, 2026-09-24). Also carried as edges in atlas layer `2026-10-31`.*

**Result.** *Dove's Guide for Church Bell Ringers*, 64,136 bells. Session 1: inside a ring bells agree to 4.5 cents; the ring stands a
median 22 cents from its named note, nearly at random until the 1950s. Session 2: measured from A = 440 alone, ring positions between the
keys are nearly even, with columns at A = 440 (5.8 %) and A = 435 (4.4 %), none at old philharmonic pitch. Session 3: by decade, the
orchestra's pitch holds a large share only in the 1970s and 80s; since 1990 the record dates mostly retunings of old bells, which keep
their key. **Declared a work, *Between the Keys*,** by the rule registered before the count.

**Postulates that carried it.** P3 (following): each session's question came out of the last one's remainder: the bell's distance (1),
the ring's (2), the decade's (3); the material moved the question twice. P4 (the refrain), unusually literal: the material is a refrain
(a ring struck each week), and the work's form is one: a steady tone as a boundary stone, rings heard against it. P6 (caution): no tower,
place or coordinate committed (metal theft named as the affected-public concern); no row of a CC BY-SA database left scratch.

**Instruments.** T5 (refrain journal), three entries: it placed the opening three times (bell → ring; "which standard" → "almost none";
the work at the beat) and was never triggered. Its finding: the components of passage it named (the heaviest bell, the concert hall's
standard, the retuning) were each a converter between the record and the sound, and each one the count later confirmed or limited. T8
(plateau format): the study page's nine sections each carry a "connects to" line, and section 9 and the work were written as entries into
sections 3 and 8 rather than a conclusion. Its failure criterion asks for a re-use the plateau form generated that a chapter form would
not: one can be named — section 8's circle was re-cut by decade into the work's raster, entered from section 3's eras — but whether a
chapter form would have prevented that is not decidable from inside; **judged not triggered, weakly.** Not choosing T4: the working log
was kept without the instrument's name, and its one recurring entry is the fault of four sessions running (a clock written ahead of
`date -u`), caught each time because the clock was read right after writing. The log did the instrument's work; the name was not needed.

**What the grammar could not do on this material.** It could not hear a bell. Every pitch is a record's measurement of one partial; the
work sounds that measurement, not a tower. It could not tell a ring retuned without record from one never touched. It could not test the
work on a listener; nobody has heard it.

**Where a return would start (a new problem, not a continuation).** The tuners' act: what a retuning removes and keeps, read from rings
whose record gives both an old and a new nominal, if such a record exists.

**Predictions:** 26 registered over three sessions (10, 8, 8): 14 held, 12 failed (5, 4, 3); two held only because they were seen before
the count (session 1, P1 and P2, reported so).
