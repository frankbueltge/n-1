# Project 20 — neighbours (session 1, 2026-10-29)

Searched before the count: the Atlas of Data Art (`https://frankbueltge.de/atlas/werke.json`, sha256 `4765ce73…`, 523 entries,
unchanged) grepped for bell, glock, campan, carillon, chime, change ringing, sonif; three web searches (standard mode), result lists and
summaries only.

- **Dove's Guide itself, "bells by nominal" and "bells by weight"** (`https://dove.cccbr.org.uk/bells-by-nominal?bells=8`,
  `https://dove.cccbr.org.uk/bells-by-weight`, `https://dove.cccbr.org.uk/rings-by-nominal`): the source charts the distribution of
  nominals per size of ring and of weights, with downloadable SVG. The nearest neighbour is the record's own display; known from the
  search summary, not yet looked at. Any page here must say what it shows that those charts do not.
- **Bill Hibbert, studies of bell tuning** (`https://www.hibberts.co.uk/stretch-tuning-of-old-style-bells/`, and the site's pages on
  Taylor's and Mears & Stainbank's tuning): 31 historic peals read for their partials; old founders tuned by strike note, so old
  nominals are stretched in the octave. A scholar's reading of chosen peals, measured by ear and program; from the search summary only,
  to be read before any work.
- **Art with bells and ringing, not with their data:** Emma Smith, *Campanologia* (itinerant sound renderings of places by their bells,
  from the search summary); Edward Bruce's woven banners of ringing methods (`https://artandchristianity.org/edward-bruce`); *Plain
  Changes* (software ringing every permutation of eight bells, `https://vimeo.com/228046100`); Bill Fontana's bell works (sound art, not
  data). None reads the record of the bells' pitches or weights.
- **Atlas of Data Art:** no entry on bells (*95 Chimes*, Debra Swack, 2005, is about string theory, not bells).
- Not found: a work that sets the note a record assigns a bell beside the sound measured from it, across many bells. To be searched
  again before any work.
- **Added 18:08, before the count:** Dove's tower pages show, in a tooltip on each bell's frequency, the bell's distance in cents from
  its named note (e.g. "E−2 cents", `https://dove.cccbr.org.uk/tower/12574`). The per-bell figure this project counts is already the
  source's. What it does not show, as far as seen: the distribution across bells, or any relation to era or founder.
