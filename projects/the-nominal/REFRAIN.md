## Entry 1 — session 1, before the count (2026-10-29, 18:07 UTC)

- **Which aspect dominates.** The hold: a new material and a first question, rung against a record I have not read. But the material is
  itself a refrain, and that is the instrument's purchase here: a ring of bells is a territory made audible ("There is a territory when
  the rhythm has expressiveness", ATP 315), struck in the same order every week, and its record names each bell by a scale laid over it.
  The record's note is a metre; the measured nominal is what the metal does.
- **Component of passage, as expected.** The tenor: the record keys the whole ring from it, so it converts every other bell's sound into
  a position. Registered as r (deviation relative to the tenor) so that the count can test whether the converter holds.
- **Opening test, stated before it can be wished.** An opening at a self-created point would be one the count makes possible and the
  record's own charts (by nominal, by weight) do not: the distance between a note named and a sound measured. If the count shows that
  distance to be noise, there is no opening there, and the journal says so rather than finds one elsewhere.
- **Sobriety.** One count, one check, one page section at most tonight.

## Entry 1, continued — after the count and the exploration (18:14 UTC)

- **What the count did to the expectation.** The tenor was registered as the component of passage (r), and it is one: taken out, bells
  agree with their ring to a median 4.5 cents, while rings stand from A = 440 Hz almost as if at random, until the 1950s. The record
  converts a ring's own pitch into a position in someone else's scale; the converter holds inside the ring and fails at its edge.
- **Aspects, coexisting.** Hold: the count and the check (20 of 20). Home: the study page, seven sections. Opening: tested below. None
  waited for another: the page was drafted while the exploration ran, and the exploration's second question (the eras) came from the
  first figure, not from a plan.
- **Opening test.** The opening named before the count (the distance between a note named and a sound measured) is there, but not where
  I placed it: it is not each bell's distance (Dove already shows it, bell by bell, as a tooltip) but the ring's. **A ring is a pitch
  of its own that the record's grammar of named notes has no place for.** That is the self-created point: neither the record's charts
  (by nominal, by weight) nor its per-bell cents show the ring standing between keys. The opening is placed there for session 2;
  P9's failure (no stretch visible in old trebles of eight) closes the other line I had, Hibbert's, as a line for this project.
- **Consolidation.** Intercalated: the tenor (one bell that gives every other its place). Interval: the gap between two named notes where
  45 % of rings stand. Superposed rhythms: the record's dates (convention years) under the record's pitches; not yet held together.
- **Sobriety.** One count, one check, one exploration, one page. The weight-pitch fit stays a paragraph; it did not open anything.

## Entry 2 — session 2, after the count and the exploration (2026-10-30, 02:10 UTC)

*Before the count this session wrote no entry here; the registration (`SESSION2.md`) carries the opening it tested. Noted so that the
journal's silence before the count is not read as a phase.*

- **Which aspect dominates.** The opening, tested from the point entry 1 placed it (the ring's own pitch), and the hold underneath it:
  the same file, unchanged by sha256, read on a new axis. Home is the page, extended by one section, not rebuilt.
- **Component of passage.** Entry 1 named the tenor, and it carried this session's count too (the ring's position is its tenor's). A
  second converter showed: **the concert hall's pitch standard**, a statement made for orchestras that crossed into towers. A = 435
  (France, 1859) holds 4.4 % of rings within a cent and a half, most among tenors cast 1925-1974 and in the Gillett foundry's rings;
  A = 440 holds 5.8 %, a third of tenors cast 1975-1999. An even spread gives 3 % at any point. The converter works, and on few.
- **Opening test.** The opening registered as two bold lines (P3: Victorian rings at old philharmonic pitch, which stood between two
  keys; P5/P6: house pitches by foundry) **did not open**: old philharmonic pitch holds 2.8 %, less than an even spread, and the large
  foundries of 1880-1949 do not hold rings at distinct pitches (their circular means all fall near −20.5 cents because the A = 435
  column pulls them, with concentrations of 0.004 to 0.28). The test **moved** the opening: not "which standard holds the rings" but
  that **almost nothing does**. Concentration 0.10 over 6,549 rings; before 1800, 0.012, indistinguishable from an even spread
  (Rayleigh p 0.70). A ring's pitch is its own in the strict sense: no hall's standard, no foundry's fork, no era fixes it. That is the
  self-created point for session 3, and the place a work could stand: the thousands of keys between keys, each a pitch that exists in
  one tower.
- **Consolidation.** Intercalated: the standards' four marks on the circle, each a dated statement from outside the towers. Interval:
  the hundred cents between two keys, now drawn whole, one cent a column. Superposed rhythms: casting years under tuning years; the
  record gives a tuning year for 1,204 of 6,549 rings, and in 1,018 of them the tenor is 50 or more years older than its tuning. Not yet
  held together: the circle reads by casting, and the sound is from the tuning.
- **Sobriety.** One count, one inside check, one exploration (two passes, the second adding the whole-hertz test and the full 1-cent
  histogram), one page section. Places were not registered and are left at one line (England, Wales, Australia).
- **Failure criterion (T5).** Not triggered: the opening test changed where the next session works (from "what holds rings" to "what a
  ring alone holds"), and the aspects coexisted (the page section was drafted while the second exploration ran).

## Entry 3 — session 3, after the count, the exploration and the work (2026-10-31, 02:14 UTC)

*Before the count this session wrote no entry here; the registration (`SESSION3.md`) carries the opening it tested and the work's form,
fixed before the count. Noted again so the silence is not read as a phase.*

- **Which aspect dominates.** Home and opening at once: the work is a territory made audible (the steady A is a boundary stone, "the
  first person to set out a boundary stone, or to make a mark", ATP 316, via KsK §4.5), and the count was made for it. The hold is the
  same file, changed by 203 bytes, giving session 2's histogram back exactly.
- **Component of passage.** Entry 2 named the concert hall's standard; tonight it is heard, not only counted. A third showed in the
  exploration: **the retuning**. A tuner who tunes an old ring keeps its key; since 1990 that act, more than casting, is what the record
  dates, and it carries the towers back between the keys.
- **Opening test.** The opening registered was "what a ring alone holds", placed as a work. It **was moved once more by the count**:
  P4 expected the beating to stop for most rings since 1990, and it returned (median 4.9 per second). The work was not reshaped to the
  failure; its form was fixed before. Its text was: the line "listen for when the wavering stops, and for when it comes back" was written
  after the count, and this entry says so. The opening was placed at a self-created point (a beat against a fork no tower hears), not where the old
  forces press (a chart of nominals, the record's own).
- **Consolidation.** Intercalated: the fork, one tone under 6,549. Interval: the gap between two keys, now a rate of wavering. Superposed
  rhythms: the record's two kinds of year (casting, tuning), heard as one order, which the page names as a reduction.
- **Sobriety.** One count, one exploration, one sound check, one page; a tone and its octave, not a bell.
- **Failure criterion (T5).** Not triggered: across three entries the opening test moved the work's place twice (bell → ring; standard
  → no standard) and placed the work; the aspects are written as coexisting in every entry.
