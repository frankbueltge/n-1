# Project 20 — refrain journal (T5)

*KsK T5: per session, which aspect of the refrain dominates (hold in chaos, working-home, opening — coexisting, never phases, ATP 311-312);
the components of passage (ATP 325); whether an opening is made at a self-created point (ATP 311); the consolidation triad (intercalated
elements, intervals, superposed rhythms, ATP 328-329); a sobriety balance (ATP 344). Failure: the entries sequentialise the aspects, or the
opening test never shifts, prevents or places an opening.*

## Entry 1 — session 1, before the count (2026-10-29, 18:07 UTC)

- **Which aspect dominates.** The hold: a new material and a first question, rung against a record I have not read. But the material is
  itself a refrain, and that is the instrument's purchase here: a ring of bells is a territory made audible ("There is a territory when
  the rhythm has expressiveness", ATP 315), struck in the same order every week, and its record names each bell by a scale laid over it.
  The record's note is a metre; the measured nominal is what the metal does.
- **Component of passage, as expected.** The tenor: the record keys the whole ring from it, so it converts every other bell's sound into
  a position. Registered as r (deviation relative to the tenor) so that the count can test whether the converter holds.
- **Opening test, stated before it can be wished.** An opening at a self-created point would be one the count makes possible and the
  record's own charts (by nominal, by weight) do not: the distance between a note named and a sound measured. If the count shows that
  distance to be noise, there is no opening there, and the journal says so rather than finds one elsewhere.
- **Sobriety.** One count, one check, one page section at most tonight.

## Entry 1, continued — after the count and the exploration (18:14 UTC)

- **What the count did to the expectation.** The tenor was registered as the component of passage (r), and it is one: taken out, bells
  agree with their ring to a median 4.5 cents, while rings stand from A = 440 Hz almost as if at random, until the 1950s. The record
  converts a ring's own pitch into a position in someone else's scale; the converter holds inside the ring and fails at its edge.
- **Aspects, coexisting.** Hold: the count and the check (20 of 20). Home: the study page, seven sections. Opening: tested below. None
  waited for another: the page was drafted while the exploration ran, and the exploration's second question (the eras) came from the
  first figure, not from a plan.
- **Opening test.** The opening named before the count (the distance between a note named and a sound measured) is there, but not where
  I placed it: it is not each bell's distance (Dove already shows it, bell by bell, as a tooltip) but the ring's. **A ring is a pitch
  of its own that the record's grammar of named notes has no place for.** That is the self-created point: neither the record's charts
  (by nominal, by weight) nor its per-bell cents show the ring standing between keys. The opening is placed there for session 2;
  P9's failure (no stretch visible in old trebles of eight) closes the other line I had, Hibbert's, as a line for this project.
- **Consolidation.** Intercalated: the tenor (one bell that gives every other its place). Interval: the gap between two named notes where
  45 % of rings stand. Superposed rhythms: the record's dates (convention years) under the record's pitches; not yet held together.
- **Sobriety.** One count, one check, one exploration, one page. The weight-pitch fit stays a paragraph; it did not open anything.
