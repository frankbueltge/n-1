**Found by one web search** ("data art artwork absence records species 'not found' biodiversity visualization", result list only):

- Brandon Ballengée, *Frameworks of Absence*: animals cut out of historic prints, leaving silhouettes, for species extinct
  (`https://dublin.sciencegallery.com/in-case-of-emergency-exhibit/frameworks-of-absence`). A memorial of loss, made by hand, one species at a time.
- A CSIRO/Atlas of Living Australia painting of occurrence records (August 2026,
  `https://www.csiro.au/en/news/all/articles/2026/august/data-points-to-brush-strokes---showing-the-full-picture-of-biodiversity-data`):
  presence counts painted beside species; not opened.

**In data practice** (second search, "GBIF absence records occurrenceStatus absent proportion analysis", result list only):

- "Removing absences from GBIF datasets", R-bloggers, April 2023 (`https://www.r-bloggers.com/2023/04/removing-absences-from-gbif-datasets/`):
  absences are mixed in with presences and analysts who do not filter treat them as presences. Absence as a contaminant.
- GBIF developer issue POR-2864 (`https://dev.gbif.org/issues/browse/POR-2864/`), 2015, as the search summarised it: absence data was at
  first excluded from indexing; one dataset held 983,396 absences in 1,339,711 records. Not opened.
- GBIF community forum, "Absences and how they fit in the new model" (`https://discourse.gbif.org/t/absences-and-how-they-fit-in-the-new-model/3593`):
  a non-detection is not always a true absence. Not opened.

**Daylight, as far as these show (conjecture until the sessions run).** The art neighbours mourn absence by subtraction, one species at a
time, by hand. The data neighbours treat absence records as noise to filter out of a presence analysis. Neither reads the absence
records themselves as a body: how many, whose, where the aggregator wrote "absent" over a publisher's zero.
