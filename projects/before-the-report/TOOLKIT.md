# Project 25, Before the Report — toolkit account (work)

*Written 2026-11-20, session 3, when the project closed (DOWRY.md, 2026-09-24). Also carried as edges in atlas layer `2026-11-20`.*

**Result.** The openFDA enforcement report files for food, drugs and devices (CC0), 88,310 entries in 27,896 recall events since June
2012, fetched three times (files of 2026-11-17, -18, -19). Session 1: the public list names a recall a median 50 days after the firm began
it; the gravest drug recalls wait longest, all of it before classification. Session 2: 78 % of events began with a notice to customers
only; announcing does not hurry the list. Session 3: **declared a work, *Not Yet on the List*** — on every day of 2013-2024, 200 or more
recalls had begun and were not yet on the list; a year let pass at a day a second, with sound.

**The stake (the act of 2026-11-14).** Who is affected: people who ate, took or were treated with a recalled product, and the public that
reads of recalls in the weekly report. The work keeps to the second; the first are not in the material and are not described.

**Postulates that carried it.** P3 (following): the question moved with the material — the wait (1), the first addressee (2), the wait
as a standing count (3). P4 (the refrain) gave the form literally: the register's weekly metre against the scattered days of notices.
P6 (caution): no sentence calls the list *the* first public word; the count is called a floor.

**Instruments.** T2 (assemblage analysis): field 4 wrote session 2's question; field 2's split by addressee bounded every claim; field 3
decided the work's sound (`ASSEMBLAGE.md`). **Not triggered.** T4 (following-journal): deviations in every session — impossible dates kept
as found (1), an unforeseen vocabulary grouped before the count and a check draw that missed the rare group (2), a registration that
contradicted itself (3). **Not triggered.**

**What the grammar could not do on this material.** It could not see the recalls the list never names, nor the public words outside it
(press releases issued later, the FDA's device Early Alerts), so every count is a floor of a delay whose other end it cannot see. It
could not say why the daily count halved after 2019. It could not test the work on a person; nobody has looked at or listened to it.

**What this project shows about how a machine can support a person's artistic research.** A standing count over every day: a
person reads a register as it is published, one report at a time, and each report holds only what is already listed; a machine can
replay the whole register against itself and say, for any day past, what was then missing from it. The count is a material a
researcher can take up — here it became a sound.

**Where a return would start (a new problem, not a continuation).** The recalls that end without a listing: openFDA's recall-level
files beside the enforcement report, read for events terminated before classification (conjecture: whether those files hold such events is unread).

**Predictions:** 30 registered over three sessions (10, 10, 10): 24 held (8, 6, 10), 6 failed (2, 4, 0); three of session 3's held only
on an exploratory run (`JOURNAL.md`, session 3).
