# Project 19, On the grid — toolkit account (work)

*Written 2026-10-29, session 4, when the project closed (DOWRY.md, 2026-09-24). Also carried as edges in atlas layer `2026-10-29-a`.*

**Result.** FoodData Central's 455,458 branded-food labels, read against the law that rounds them (21 CFR 101.9). Session 1: only 71.5 %
of declared calorie values are numbers a label may print; most of the rest are a lawful number sent to 100 g and back. Session 2: all but
116 of 416,781 main-source values are the per-100 g amount said again; the serving size decides survival. Session 3: run backwards, 96.8 %
lead to exactly one lawful number, 100,618 can be given back; the outside check answered none. Session 4: 108 photographs of packages
read blind; of 77 legible given-back, 64 print the number given back and none the database's. **Declared a work, *As Printed*,** by the
rule registered before the reading.

**Postulates that carried it.** P3 (following): each session's question came from the last one's remainder: how far off the grid (1),
what decides it (2), whether it can be undone (3), whether the undoing reaches a package (4). P2 (map, not copy): the work does not show the
displacement again; it gives the label back and keeps the database's number beneath it. P6 (caution): no firm named or judged; Open Food
Facts' values and photographs not copied; four foods whose numbers were seen before the reading left out of it.

**Instruments.** T3 (pragmatics audit), four entries: it moved the design three times (session 2 to the record's own sentence; session 3
from showing to restoring; session 4 to a body, the package, rather than a second database) and was never triggered. Its finding: the
order-word here is "per serving", and the work's sentence ("this label printed 150") was avowed until a package referred it. T4
(following-journal), four sessions: every session registered detours (binary versus decimal rounding; the outside database's form; the
photograph address not served), and three clocks were written ahead of `date -u` and corrected. **Their finding about the toolkit:** T3
decided what would count as the referent; T4's record of faults is where the practice's own working cracked, and it cracked at its clock.

**What the grammar could not do on this material.** It could not see a package except through someone else's photograph: the reading is
of 108 photographs, not of the 96 panels the work draws. It could not say where the 13,396 unrestorable values came from (class D, the
record's only numbers not of its own arithmetic). It could not tell a reformulated product from a wrong number when a photograph disagrees.
And it could not test reception on a stranger.

**Where a return would start (a new problem, not a continuation).** The labels that print what the law does not allow (118, 175 kcal; 11
and 96 mg sodium seen among 105): how often a package itself is off the grid, read from photographs at scale.

**Predictions:** 30 registered over four sessions (8, 8, 8, 6): 20 held, 6 failed, 3 not made (session 3's check answered none), and 1
failed as registered and held under a correction stated beside it (session 2, P1).
