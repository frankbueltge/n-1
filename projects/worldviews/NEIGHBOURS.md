## In the Atlas of Data Art

- **Hagit Keysar and Ariel Caine (with Barak Brinker), *Terra Ex Machina* (2026)**, Prix Ars Electronica: crashes drones into an
  invisible algorithmic geofence over Jerusalem's Old City and rebuilds aerial imagery with residents, "to make the boundary and its data
  politics legible". Nearest in problem: a boundary that exists only as data. One boundary, met in the body; not a count across views.
- **Postcommodity, *Repellent Fence* (2015)**: a two-mile line of balloons straddling the US/Mexico border, re-joining land a border
  divided. A border answered on the ground.
- **Christian Marc Schmidt, *1833 Chicago City Limits* (2006)**, Rhizome ArtBase: photographs walking former political boundaries within
  a city. Boundaries that were, traced on foot.
- **Forensic Architecture**, several cartographic investigations in the Atlas: maps as evidence for a claim. This project makes no claim
  about any territory, which is the line between them.

## Outside the Atlas (one search, 2026-10-19; not seen)

- **Quartz (2014), "See how borders change on Google Maps depending on where you view them"**
  (`https://qz.com/224821/see-how-borders-change-on-google-maps-depending-on-where-you-view-them`): per the search summary, a team at a
  Knight-Mozilla OpenNews hackathon wrote software comparing Google Maps' borders across viewer locations and documented how twelve
  disputed areas are drawn. **The nearest neighbour found**: the same question, asked of another publisher, area by area, for twelve
  named areas. Journalism and a hack, not declared art.
- Press on Google Maps' local versions of Kashmir and Arunachal Pradesh (e.g. `https://www.tbsnews.net/international/google-redraws-borders-maps-based-whos-viewing-45067?amp`):
  the phenomenon reported, not counted.

## Daylight (provisional, session 1)

The Quartz hack compares named disputed areas in one publisher's tiles. This project counts **all land** in 34 published editions of one
open map, without choosing in advance which areas are disputed: the contested land is whatever the editions disagree on, including what no
list of disputes would name. Whether that is daylight enough is asked at close, with the work, not now.
