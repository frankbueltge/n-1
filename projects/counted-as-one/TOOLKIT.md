# Project 26, Counted as One — toolkit account (work)

*Written 2026-11-24, session 3, when the project closed (DOWRY.md, 2026-09-24). Also carried as edges in atlas layer `2026-11-24`.*

**Result.** The Environment Agency's Event Duration Monitoring start/stop data for 2025 and the 2025 storm-overflow annual return (Open
Government Licence), fetched three times with identical hashes. Session 1: the 12/24 rule makes 1,120,341 discharges into 293,036
counted spills, and at the investigation trigger the count does not order the hours (1 h 16 min counted 44; 567 h counted 27). Session
2: of the 206 overflows counted under 30 that discharged longer than the median one over it, 3.0 % of the hours fall in the bathing
season. Session 3: **declared a work, *Out of Season*** — their years as year planners; the ink in December and January, the season's frame
nearly empty, the dots of the count spread across both.

**The stake (the act of 2026-11-14).** Who is affected: people who swim, paddle, fish or play downstream, the living things of those
waters, and the public told one number per overflow. The work keeps to what the record holds for the last; the first two are not in the
material, and the page says it cannot show whether a discharge reached them.

**Postulates that carried it.** P3 (following): each session's question came from the last session's surprise — the trigger line (1), the
calendar of the long discharges (2), the form of the year (3). P2 (map, not copy): the work is a map in the most literal sense the
practice has made — a grid the visitor already reads, with two other grids drawn onto it. P6 (caution): "not sent for investigation" was
struck for what the record says ("High spill threshold not met"), the two-year arm of the trigger named as untested, a beach never said
to be reached.

**Instruments.** T7 (smooth/striated audit): located the problem at the trigger line (1), then in the meeting of two calendars (2), then
chose the form as a striation that propagates rather than a ranking that overcodes (3) (`AUDIT.md`). Every session's figure was
decided by it. **Not triggered**: the balance came out two-sided each time. T4 (following-journal): deviations in every session — a
library that would not install, blank rows counted as malformed (1); a check done by awk where "by hand" was registered (2); a data field
added after registration, a weak check draw, a refused server (3). **Not triggered.**

**What the grammar could not do on this material.** It could not follow the water: the record ends at the outfall, and nothing in the
toolkit reaches downstream to a beach or a body. It could not test the trigger's second arm (20 a year over two years) on one year, nor
explain the recount's differences at the year's edge. T7's "who striates?" had a ready answer — a statute and an agency's appendix — and
the instrument was strongest where the answer was given, weakest at asking what the discharge does, which is not a question of space and
count. Nobody has looked at the work.

**What this project shows about how a machine can support a person's artistic research.** A rule re-run over everything it was meant to
summarise: the agency's counting rule applied again to every recorded start and stop, so that what it lets pass can be listed by name
and laid out day by day. A person can read the published count; a machine can recount a whole year from the raw record and hand the
researcher the cases where the count and the hours disagree — material a person can then take somewhere a machine cannot, to the water.

**Where a return would start (a new problem, not a continuation).** The 2024 start/stop data beside 2025's: the trigger's two-year arm, and
whether the recount's year-edge differences come from a count carried across New Year (conjecture). Or the bathing-water sampling record
of the 19 bathing-named overflows' waters, which is another material and another keeper.

**Predictions:** 29 registered over three sessions (10, 9, 10): 23 held (8, 6, 9), 6 failed (2, 3, 1).
