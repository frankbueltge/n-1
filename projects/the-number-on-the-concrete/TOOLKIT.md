# Project 21, The number on the concrete — toolkit account (work)

*Written 2026-11-03, session 3, when the project closed (DOWRY.md, 2026-09-24). Also carried as edges in atlas layer `2026-11-03`.*

**Result.** OurAirports' runways (public domain) against IGRF-14, 1900-2030. Session 1: 28 % of 12,178 runways carry a number the
field has left behind, 93 % trailing it; the numbers fit the field of 2002. Session 2: in the United States the lag is mostly the
official record's (85 %), and the regulator dates its declared field on five-year marks (median 2000; 35 airports declare 2030).
Session 3: square by square, 154 of 262 places carry a year, half 2003 or earlier. **Declared a work, *The Year on the Concrete*,** by the
rule registered before the count.

**Postulates that carried it.** P3 (following): the question moved three times with the material — how many are wrong (1), whose lag
(2), what year each place says (3); the turn from "how many" to "which year" came out of session 1's count, not its registration. P2 (map,
not tracing), unusually literal: the work is a map drawn by the marks it maps, with no base map under it. P6 (caution): no airport or
runway on any page, squares under ten runways not drawn, "not for navigation" on every page; a key NOAA's own page sends was not used
(session 1).

**Instruments.** T2 (assemblage analysis), three entries. Field 4 was never empty, and each entry found a movement the next session
followed: a statement outlived by its reference (1); not one lag but rhythms superposed (2); a collective statement — a year — that no
body made (3). It changed one decision that can be named: entry 2's "superposition" put the United States' four clocks on the work's page
as one sentence instead of a second map. **Not triggered.** T4 (following-journal), three sessions, written as the work went (clock
times read from `date -u`): a detour in every session (a time-zone candidate dropped before opening; a declared year of 2030 that stopped
an exploration; a crashed run and a weak check). **Not triggered.** Its practical yield is a habit: a synthetic run before the count,
begun after session 2's sign error, which tonight passed and then did not prevent a different fault (a type error at writing), caught
because the fix was pushed before the second run.

**What the grammar could not do on this material.** It could not see paint. Every number is a record's; whether the concrete agrees, no
session could say. It could not tell a number left behind from one kept on purpose (a regulator's threshold, a cost). It could not test
the work on a person; nobody has looked at it.

**Where a return would start (a new problem, not a continuation).** The renumbering itself as an event: a record of notices (NOTAMs or
national AIP amendments) that dates when numbers were put right, read against the year the field crossed the rounding line.

**Predictions:** 30 registered over three sessions (11, 10, 9): 24 held, 6 failed (3, 2, 1; session 3's failure P7: the world's share
right for the field of 1950 was 62.9 %, not ≤ 55 %). Session 3's P1 was a check on numbers seen before, reported so.
